Knowledge Warranty, design session, 29 September 2026

Five design directions for a product that has to be believed.

A working session between the agency and the Knowledge Warranty team. Six directions were pitched, one was cut, five are built out below as a design system and a UI kit each. Same colours in all five.

The brief

What we agreed before anyone drew anything.

Who uses itSynchrone engineers and mission leads, on a laptop, often mid-incident. They want the answer, the proof, and the minute.
What it must sayEvery answer is checkable. When it does not know, it says so. Current and replaced decisions are never confused.
ConstraintsKeep the palette. Keep Tailwind tokens so the swap is a theme change, not a rewrite. Readable in a 1360 x 900 video frame.

The session

Condensed from the working meeting. CEO, CTO, lead engineer, product designer, and us.

1. What is wrong today
CEO

It works, but it looks like every other SaaS dashboard. White cards on grey, a black band on top, rounded everything. Nobody would guess it is the thing that tells you whether a decision is still true.

Designer

The AmplifyME borrowing gave us a look, not a language. Heavy uppercase headlines fight the evidence. The quote from the meeting is the most important thing on the page and it is set smaller than the heading above it.

Engineer

From my side: the trace panel is the product. Six steps, real scores. Right now it is a grey table under a link. If the redesign makes that feel like an afterthought again, I will fight it.

CTO

Two hard rules. We keep the colours, teal and amber already mean current and replaced in the data. And nothing that needs a new component library. Fonts, tokens, spacing, borders. That is the budget.

Agency

Agreed. So the question is not which colours. It is what the product is, visually: a file, a console, an index, a tape, or a log. Each of those is a different promise to the user. We will pitch six and argue.

2. The pitches
Agency

One, Evidence File. The product is a dossier. Serif for everything spoken, monospace for timecodes and IDs, hairline rules instead of cards, red used like a stamp on paper. Every answer reads like an exhibit.

CEO

That is the trust story in one image. My worry is it feels like a law firm, not a tech product.

Designer

It feels like a law firm if we add ornaments. Without them it is closer to a good newspaper. The Financial Times does not look old.

Agency

Two, Control Room. Dark first. The archive is a live system: red is the on-air light, teal and amber are status lamps, timecodes everywhere, dense panels. Built for the engineer mid-incident.

Engineer

I like it more than I should. The trace would finally look like what it is.

CTO

Dark UIs record badly on video and the serif quotes will lose contrast. If we pick it, the answer card has to stay light.

Agency

Three, Swiss Index. International typographic style. One grotesk, a strict 12 column grid, very large numbers, flush left, red only as small square markers. No boxes: whitespace and rules do the grouping.

Designer

This is the one that fixes the "centered and cramped" complaint for free. The grid forces a use of the full width.

CEO

It is the one that looks most like a real company. It is also the one I could imagine forgetting.

Agency

Four, Studio Tape. Audio native. Every recording is a track with a waveform, every citation is a timecode chip you can press, the playhead is the red. Softer shapes, warmer surfaces. It says "we listened".

CTO

The waveform means we store peaks per recording. That is one numpy pass at index time, so fine, but it is the only direction that needs new data.

Agency

Five, Plain Log. Monospace everywhere, black 1.5 px borders, square corners, tables first. The answer and its trace read like a log you can audit. Brutal and honest.

Engineer

Engineers at Synchrone would trust this on sight.

CEO

Mission leads and the direction would not. Keep it in the set, but it is a hard sell for the final.

Agency

Six, Soft Glass. Frosted panels, blurred gradients from the red, large radii, floating everything.

Designer

No. That is the look we are trying to leave. Blur behind a quote makes it harder to read, and gradients from the signal red turn it pink.

CTO

Also backdrop-filter on a long transcript page is a scroll performance problem. Cut.

3. Rules every direction must follow
Designer

The spoken quote is the biggest text in an answer, bigger than our own sentence. Teal and amber only ever mean current and replaced. Red is never used for an error that is not a refusal or a failure.

Engineer

The six trace steps are visible in every kit, with "Not applicable" as a first class state, not a greyed row.

CTO

Each file ends with an implementation note: which fonts, which Tailwind tokens change, what gets deleted. If a direction cannot be described in that note, it is too expensive.

CEO

And every kit shows the same screen, the deployment question, so we compare like for like.

Our recommendation: 01 Evidence File, with the grid from 03.

The product's one promise is "you can check this". Evidence File is the only direction where that promise is the visual idea itself: the quote is the exhibit, the timecode is the reference, the stamp says current or replaced. It is also cheap: two fonts and borders instead of shadows.

Its weakness is layout, which is exactly Swiss Index's strength. Take the 12 column grid and the large numbers from 03 for the dashboard and the Quality report, and keep Evidence File for everything that shows what was said. If the team wants one direction untouched, pick 03: it is the safest on video.

Knowledge Warranty, Team 9. Design directions are proposals; nothing here changes the app until one is picked.
