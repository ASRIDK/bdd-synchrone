BUSINESS DEEP DIVE I · 14 SEP – 2 OCT 2026
Making a company's own recordings usable again
Synchrone × Dataiku × Albert School.
The case
You have a two-hour lecture recording open. You know the thing you need is in there,
somewhere in the middle. You drag the progress bar, land too late, drag back, land too early,
and give up.
Now scale that up to a company.
Meetings, internal training, an engineer explaining to a colleague how he solved something. Thousands
of hours, recorded and never opened again. Two years later the same problem comes back, the
engineer has left, and nothing tells anyone that the answer is sitting at minute 34 of a file called
"Weekly product sync".
So the work is done a second time, from nothing. It takes three weeks.
Synchrone is an engineering consultancy. What its clients buy is what its engineers know. When one of
them moves on, what is left is whatever happened to be recorded.
For the next three weeks, Synchrone is your client.
THE BRIEF
Build them something that would turn their recordings into value, and that they
would pay for.
Why this is hard
Getting an answer out of one recording is easy. You watch it. At a thousand hours, nothing is easy any
more.
What does it cost to process a thousand hours, and is what you get back worth that?
When the answer could be in any of a thousand files, how do you land on the right one, and on the
right minute inside it?
How do you know the answer you are handed is right, when nobody is going to watch the source to
check?
What can all of it answer, and what can it not? Who finds out, and how?

So what is asked of you is not to make something run. It is to build something that would hold at that
size, for a price they would accept, and to show both.
Almost nothing is decided
Who in the company this is for. What value means for them. What shape the thing takes. What counts as
good enough, what it may cost, and whether it is worth doing at all.
None of that is settled, by Synchrone or by us. It is yours to settle, and yours to defend.

How you are graded
Every grade is out of 100. The percentages are how much each one counts.
Reverse brief
15% · Submission due Wed 16 Sep 23:59 · one per team on Inside
FORMAT A PDF of 2 pages, named Team X - Reverse brief.pdf.
CONTENT 1. The problem as you understand it, who in the company has it, and what it costs them
today.
2. The routes you could take to solve it, and what you think of each.
3. What a good result looks like: the cases you will test it on, and the outcome you
expect for each.
4. What fixing it would be worth at full scale, what it would cost to build and to run,
and where each figure comes from.
5. What you expect to be hard, and who owns what in the team.
GRADE Problem framing 25%
Whether you state the problem better than it was given to you, who has it, and what
it costs them today.
Options and judgement 25%
How many real routes you considered, and whether you have a view on each of
them.
What a good result is 20%
Whether your cases and expected outcomes are specific enough to be checked.
Value and cost 20%
Whether your estimate of what fixing it is worth and what it would cost holds up,
and where each figure comes from.
Risks and ownership 10%
Whether you name the real difficulties, and whether each person owns something.
FEEDBACK Your grade and a written feedback on Inside.

On the Grill 1
15% · Defence on Fri 18 Sep · one grade per team
FORMAT 12 minutes in front of a Synchrone engineer: 5 where you present on slides, 7 where
they challenge you.
CONTENT Where you are: what you have found in the data, the approach you settled on, and what
you have already tried.
GRADE Knowledge of the data 20%
Whether you have been into the data yourself, and can say what is in it.
Relevance for the client 15%
Whether what you propose would matter to the client, who would use it, and why
they would.
What you have already tried 25%
How much ground you covered, and what you learned from each attempt. The ones
that failed count as much as the ones that worked.
Holding up under questioning 30%
Whether your answers stand when pushed, and whether you say you do not know
when you do not.
Clarity 10%
Whether you get to the point in the 5 minutes you have.
FEEDBACK What the engineer tells you in the room. Your grade on Inside afterwards.

The demo
30% · Submission due Tue 29 Sep 23:59 · one per team on Inside
FORMAT The video, 4 minutes maximum, as a Loom link or as a file named Team X - Video.
Nothing past 4 minutes is watched.
The slides, as a PDF named Team X - Slides.pdf.
CONTENT The video is a live demonstration of your solution and of how it works, editing allowed.
The slides are the ones you would present at the final.
GRADE Only the video is graded.
What it does 25%
How much of the problem it solves, shown running end to end on screen.
How it works 25%
Whether you show what happens between the data going in and the result coming
out, step by step.
The cases you run it on 20%
Whether you run it on cases that could break it, and show what happens when they
do.
Quality of what it produces 20%
Whether what comes out is right and useful, judged on what you show.
Clarity 10%
Whether someone watching it alone, once, can follow it.
FEEDBACK Your grade and a written feedback on Inside.

Peer review
20% · Submission due Thu 1 Oct 23:59 · one per student on Inside
FORMAT Individual: each of you submits your own. What you write about a teammate reaches
that teammate with your name on it, and nobody else on the team sees it.
CONTENT You split 100 points between your teammates, according to how much each of them
contributed compared with the others, and you write a few lines about each of them.
You do not rate yourself: your own points are the ones your teammates give you. An
equal split is 100 divided by the number of teammates you have, so 25 if you have four
of them.
GRADE Your grade is the average of what your teammates gave you, as a percentage of an equal
split, capped at 100. With four teammates an equal split is 25: 25 points gives you 100, 20
gives you 80, 10 gives you 40, and nothing gives you 0. The points your teammates gave
you only count if you submitted yours: otherwise your grade is 0.
FEEDBACK The points and the lines each of your teammates gave you, with their names.
